# Fireship (The Code Report): how 15 recent long-form videos hold viewers and teach

## Short answer
All 15 videos use one template. The title makes a dramatic claim and usually ends in "...". The thumbnail is a dark frame with a 2–4 word red and cream label next to a face or character. The video opens on a time stamp ("Last week…", "Yesterday…") and a cold-open joke. None of them starts on the thumbnail image. Viewers are held by a chain of "But…" turns that arrive every 15–40 seconds, about 200 words per minute of dry jokes, and a picture that changes every 1.6 seconds. The promise is named early (median 8 s) but usually paid off late (median 220 s, about 69% of the way in). After that come a sponsor read tied to the topic and a short sign-off. The teaching is real but light: about 7 ideas per video, mostly carried by quick definitions and concrete examples or stories. Hard mechanisms are often compressed or skipped.

What the numbers mean:
- **The picture changes about twice as often as the shot.** There are roughly 29.5 visual changes per minute against 13.1 cuts per minute. Stickers, captions, arrows and screenshot pop-ins change the frame about every 1.6 s inside shots that last about 3.2 s.
- **Speech starts at once.** Narration begins at second 0 in 14 videos and at 0.1 s in V8.
- **The opening is slightly slower.** Speech runs a little under the overall pace in the first 3–10 s (median 180–186 wpm against 203 overall), then speeds up to full rate by 30–60 s.
- **The opening seconds are not where the cuts are.** Five videos have no cut at all in the first 3 s (V2, V3, V8, V9, V13). V7 is the slowest cutter (8.3 per minute) but still keeps visual changes high (30.3 per minute).
- **The pace barely changes between videos.** Speech varies by only about 19 wpm across all 15.

## Packaging (title + thumbnail)
- **The title is a news claim with a trailing ellipsis: 13 of 15.** Examples: "The runtime wars are over... and Node won" [V5], "DHH has gone completely off the rails..." [V12]. The other 2 are yes/no questions: "Did Google just dethrone OpenAI and Anthropic?" [V4] and the military-secret question [V11].
- **Big stakes or superlatives: most titles.** Examples: "most vulnerable week in internet history" [V2], "most expensive 33 hours" [V14], "Every app on Earth" [V7], "$6.3 billion" [V8].
- **The title holds back the name the thumbnail gives.** The thumbnail names the product and the title hides it in V1 ("new frontier model" against "KIMI K3.1"), V4 (Gemini 4), V7 (Reverse Engineer Anything / Adobe), V10 and V15 ("ex-OpenAI researcher" against "JEV"). The gap is between the two texts. The thumbnail never repeats the title.
- **Thumbnail layout is fixed: 15 of 15 are on a near-black or charcoal background.** The red and cream headline sits on the left. A face or character sits on the right in 14 of 15; V13 shows a hand holding a device instead. A product or company logo appears in 12 of 15. The "THE CODE REPORT" badge is noted in 13 of 15.
- **Thumbnail text is 2–4 words.** Examples: "THE internet's BURNING" [V2], "OpenAI DOUBLE AGENT" [V6], "BANNED by OpenAI" [V9]. In 5 of 15 a small lowercase tag sets the format: "first look" [V1] [V15], "recap" [V13], "revisited" [V3], "(again)" [V12].
- **The curiosity gap is one of three types:**
  - Is it real or hype? V1, V4, V8, V11, V12, V15.
  - What happened, and how? V2, V6, V9, V14.
  - Which one or what? V3, V10, V13.
- **Mismatch.** V3's thumbnail (a man with a weight plate, "PROGRAMMER STEREO TYPES revisited") has little to do with its "dying out" title.

## The first 3, 10 and 30 seconds
The order is almost always the same:

1. **0–3 s: time stamp plus cold open.** 13 of 15 open on a time word: "Last week" in 9 videos, "Yesterday" in 2 (V6, V8), "Earlier this week/month" in 2 (V13, V14). In 10 of 15 the time word also appears as a black text card such as "LAST WEEK..." [V1 0s] [V9 2s] [V12 1s] or "YESTERDAY..." [V6 0s] [V8 0s]. The two exceptions use a parody frame: nostalgic stock footage with "THE GOOD OL' DAYS..." [V2 0s], and a nature-documentary voice, "The programmer." [V3 0s].
2. **3–10 s: a joke or absurd claim instead of the topic.** Examples: "gas changed the world forever, twice" [V4 0s], a monitor-smashing clip about Adobe [V7 0s], a Metaverse real-estate certification joke [V13 0s], a chess analogy for "kibitz" [V1 8s].
3. **10–30 s: the subject is named and the stakes stack up.** Examples: the NSA telling PHP users to go dark [V2 13s], Gemini 4 Argon named [V4 20s], "banned its creator's account twice" [V9 24s], Kimi K3.1 revealed [V1 30s].
4. **30–60 s: a spoken roadmap, then the dateline.** The roadmap sounds like "In today's video…" or "we'll find out…" [V2 38s] [V5 36s] [V8 98s] [V12 79s]. The dateline sounds like "It is September 25th, 2026, and you're watching the Code Report" [V13 55s].

**The opening matches the thumbnail in only 1 of 15 videos** (V10, where Sam Altman is on screen within the first second). The other 14 open on something else and reach the thumbnail's subject later.

**When the promise is shown and paid off:**
- Shown: median **8 s** (range 0–65 s). The latest are V11 at 65 s and V7 at 45 s.
- Paid off: median **220 s** (range 4–345 s), about 69% of the runtime.
- Late payoff (after the halfway point) in 10 of 15, for example the horse verdict [V1 326s], the alignment verdict [V4 345s] and "promising, but not perfect" [V11 275s].
- Early payoff (in the first 10 s) in 3 of 15: [V5 8s], [V13 8s], and V10. V10's record says 4 s, but its beat list puts the actual money payoff ("sign in with ChatGPT") at [V10 260s], so V10 is probably a late payoff too.
- V2 and V15 sit in between: [V2 75s], [V15 100s].

**Self-introduction.** The creator never introduces himself by name in any of the 15. The only identity moment is a show name and date. The records log it in 9 of 15, median 53 s (range 37–94 s). V5 also says the show name at about 48 s. In V7 the name appears only in the outro. V3, V9, V14 and V15 have no recorded intro at all.

## Structure and re-hooks
**The common beat sequence:**
1. Hook
2. Subject named, stakes raised
3. Promise or roadmap
4. Dateline and show name
5. Backstory or context
6. Explanation blocks, each ending on a "But…" twist
7. Hands-on test or key reveal
8. Verdict (the payoff)
9. Sponsor bridge
10. CTA
11. "See you in the next one" sign-off

**What forms the spine:**
- A chronology of real events: V2, V5, V6, V14.
- A list of items: stereotypes in V3, skills in V12, announcements in V10 and V13.
- A test the creator runs himself, in 4 of 15: "Thoroughbench" [V1 235s], the 3D horse test [V4 275s], reverse-engineering Premiere [V7 152s], the Horsetinder leak test [V11 230s].

**Re-hook cadence.** The descriptions put new stakes or a turn every 10–25 s in the fastest videos (V2, V6, V10, V14), every 15–40 s in most, and every 30–80 s in the slowest (V4, V7, V15). The device is almost always a pivot word:
- "But then, their momentum died." [V5 105s]
- "But that was only the beginning" [V2 160s]
- "But now it's time to try Kimmy K3.1." [V1 271s]
- "But something doesn't add up here" [V12 288s]

V4 adds a "Viewer discretion is advised" warning before its reveal [V4 300s].

**Open loops.** Every video opens a question it carries until the payoff. 13 of 15 also leave at least one loop unanswered:
- who the spy worked for [V6 50s]
- whether Reverse Engineer Anything ends software as we know it [V7 70s]
- how Jev works [V15 228s]

V2 and V5 close all their loops. Running callbacks fill the gaps between pivots. "Trust Me Bro" benchmarks or polls appear in 5 of 15 (V1, V4, V7, V8, V12). A horse or donkey running gag appears in 6 (V1, V4, V7, V10, V11, V15).

**Sponsor placement.** In 14 of 15 the sponsor read starts after the payoff, at a median of about 294 s (85% of the runtime; range 257–355 s). It is bridged from the topic, for example "no matter which model you choose" [V8 316s] or "But no matter which stereotype you identify as" [V3 290s]. The exception is V11, where the sponsor's product is also the subject of the video and is named at [V11 58s]. Every video ends with a sponsor CTA. Only V12 asks viewers to comment [V12 275s]. None of the records shows a subscribe prompt.

## How it teaches
**Volume.** The records log 95 ideas across the 15 videos: median 7 per video, range 3 [V14] to 8 [V2, V3, V11, V13].

**How ideas are carried (95 total):**

| Vehicle | Ideas | Videos using it at least once |
|---|---|---|
| Definition | 32 | 15 |
| Example | 20 | 13 |
| Story | 19 | 11 |
| Analogy | 7 | 5 |
| Demo | 7 | 6 |
| Named misconception | 5 | — |
| Visual | 3 | — |
| Question | 2 | — |

A story or real case carries the video in 13 of 15: news timelines, the M assistant history [V13 72s], the creator's own Horsetinder app. V9 and V10 have no story vehicle.

**The usual order of moves:** news hook → name the thing → one-sentence plain-English definition → concrete example, number or joke → caveat or verdict. Examples:
- Distillation is named, then "Basically, with distillation, you use a bigger teacher model…" [V9 158s].
- Military classification comes first as an analogy, then the session-taint rule, then a medical-data example, then a test on the creator's own app [V11 150s].
- Durable objects get one clause: "tiny single-threaded servers that each get their own private SQLite database" [V5 172s].

**Jargon and prior knowledge.** All 15 assume a viewer who already knows developer and AI tooling. Most terms go unexplained: harness, MoE, MCP, tokens, zero-shot, Linux VM. Only the term the story hinges on gets a gloss. Examples: the HTTPS lock [V2 212s], a board of directors [V14 60s] (introduced with "If you're unfamiliar with how most tech companies work"), native binaries [V7 170s].

**Misconceptions.** 13 of 15 correct at least one common belief, usually with "But…". Examples:
- "stay up to date" is safe, until LiteLLM [V2 195s]
- block lists and a "babysitter" second LLM [V11 105s] [V11 120s]
- type-safe output does not mean correct output [V15 189s]
- reading the non-bold benchmark numbers [V4 145s]

V3 and V10 list none.

**"Think first" moments.** All 15 have them: a rhetorical question held open before the answer. Examples: "So is it really true…?" [V7 72s], "But you know who is anti-AI?" [V3 260s], "Sounds too good to be true" [V15 70s], "I pushed play on the animation" [V1 300s].

**The hardest part** is handled in two ways:
- **In layers**, in V2, V9, V10, V11 and V14. PHP2Shell is split into two flaws plus a file-upload timing example [V2 81s] [V2 120s]. The Decisions API gets a before/after contrast and a code screenshot with red boxes [V10 215s]. Board power is taught twice, once at [V14 60s] and again with the 84% figure at [V14 215s].
- **Compressed or skipped**, in V3, V7, V12, V13 and V15. The Premiere pseudocode is called "garbage" and never shown [V7 235s]. Jev's architecture gets "nobody knows for sure" [V15 228s]. The key skill claim in V12 is one abstract sentence [V12 292s].

**Recaps.** Only 5 of 15 have one, and it is short:
- a mini-recap [V2 285s]
- the "Node is the only one left" summary [V5 285s]
- a light wrap-up [V6 255s]
- a tiered "which should you use" decision [V8 302s]
- a verdict with numbers [V11 285s]

## Script language
**Tone.** Dry, sardonic, meme-literate news commentary from a developer to developers. It is skeptical of hype, including the topic's own claims, and mixes insults, profanity (V7, V12) and self-deprecation.

**Techniques:**
- A time-anchored cold open.
- "But" and "Then" pivots as section joints.
- Concrete numbers as stakes and punchlines: 48 hours, $23 board, 84%, 4.20 score.
- Invented labels and callbacks: Thoroughbench, Trust Me Bro, Horsetinder.
- Direct "you" address.
- Long run-on setup sentences (median 21.5 words) closed by very short verdicts.

**Example lines (verbatim):**
- "But then, their momentum died." [V5 105s]
- "The animations are absolutely terrible." [V1 300s]
- "And on October 5th, he was back." [V6 210s]
- "So it's basically just a bot with a B flipped around." [V10 80s]
- "Chat models are trained to police human raters, and humans love confidence, which is how we got models that are wrong with the confidence of Kanye." [V15 215s]
- "Good work if you can get it." [V14]

## Limits
- **Sample.** One channel, its latest 15 long-form videos (303–403 s each), as of 2026-11-03. The patterns may not hold for older videos or for other channels.
- **What code measured.** Cuts, shot lengths, visual changes, speech rate, first speech second and sentence length are code-measured and reliable.
- **What a model described.** Beats, promise timings, re-hook cadence, open loops, teaching vehicles, misconceptions and tone come from a model reading transcripts, thumbnails and sampled frames. Timings there are approximate, and labels such as "definition" against "example" are judgment calls. At least one record is internally inconsistent: V10's promise-delivery time is 4 s in one field and 260 s in its beats.
- **No retention data.** YouTube does not publish audience retention, so "holds viewers" here means design intent inferred from structure, not measured watch time. View counts (0.9M–6.6M) are not linked to any single technique.
