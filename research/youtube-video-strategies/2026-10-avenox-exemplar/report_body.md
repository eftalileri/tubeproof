# Avenox: a script that talks like a friend and gives firm verdicts

## Short answer
All 10 videos start speaking at 0.0 s. None of them opens with a greeting or a self-introduction. The first line is a claim, a stake or a reminder of what the audience already knows ("Biliyorsunuz…", "Bildiğiniz üzere…"). The click comes from short, two-tone thumbnail text next to a deadpan yellow mascot (9 of 10). The promise is almost always a question or a bold claim about the newest AI models. Viewers are kept by very short spoken sentences (median 6 words, about 187 words per minute) and a near-constant "Bakın" pivot (9 of 10). New stakes arrive every 25–60 s in the shorter videos. The narrator gives firm verdicts but hedges them with "bence" and backs them with his own tests and numbers. The style is very consistent across the channel. What changes is the format (news roundup, test, guide, opinion talk), and in the long videos the pace of re-hooks slows.

## Packaging (title + thumbnail)
- **Mascot with a deadpan or sceptical face: 9 of 10.** A yellow lemon/banana/bean mascot appears in V2–V10, usually holding or pointing at the subject: a censor strip [V2], a GitHub card [V7], a magnifying glass over the Gemini logo [V8], an open OpenAI device [V5]. V1 uses a yellow halftone caricature with a top hat in the same style.
- **Black or dark background with short bold text, often white and yellow: 10 of 10 are dark.** The text is 2–4 words: "SANSÜR KALKTI" [V2], "BİREBİR AYNI" [V9], "BİZE VERMİYORLAR" [V10], "DOĞRU İŞE DOĞRU MODEL" [V6].
- **Question in the title: 5 of 10** (V1, V4, V5, V8, V10). **Question mark in the thumbnail text: 3 of 10** ("PARA KAZANMAK?" [V1], "OPENAI'DA CASUS?" [V5], "GEMINI 4 ARGON?" [V8]).
- **Kinds of promise:** test or comparison (V2, V6, V8), step-by-step guide (V3, V4), weekly news roundup branded "Yapay Zeka Gündemi" (V5, V10), trend explainer (V7, V9), opinion talk (V1). Reverse engineering is the topic of 4 of 10 (V2, V4, V7, V9), which makes it a series.
- **Curiosity gap:** usually "is the hype true?" or "who or what is behind this?". Examples: "Övüldüğü Kadar İyi mi?" [V8], and "Kazanan Tek Model Değil, Bir İkili" [V6], which names the answer type but not the pair.
- The thumbnail scene matches the first frame in only **1 of 10** (V8).

## The first 3, 10 and 30 seconds
- **0–3 s:** speech starts at 0.0 s in 10 of 10, with no greeting in any video. The opening devices are:
  - a bold claim or stake: 6 of 10 (V2, V3, V5, V7, V9, V10). Examples: "Bu sahnelerin hepsini son 3 haftada yapay zeka ile yaptılar." [V9 0s] and "Bu hafta Claude'un bir agent'ı polise sahte cinayet…" [V10 0s].
  - an appeal to the audience's shared knowledge: 3 of 10 (V4 "çok ilgi duydunuz" [V4 0s], V5 "Biliyorsunuz" [V5 0s], V8 "Bildiğiniz üzere" [V8 0s]).
  - an accusation aimed at "sen": V1, "Yapay zeka ile uğraşıyorsun. Her yeni modeli ilk deneyen sensin." [V1 0s].
  - a list of model names: V6 [V6 0s].
- **3–10 s:** the narrator stacks a second stake or names the problem. Examples: the agent refusing the task [V4 12s], Google's sworn testimony [V10 12s], a number (2,500 → 30k merges) [V5 8s].
- **10–30 s:** he states the scope or roadmap ("54 events" [V10 30s], "no tutorial, podcast-style" [V1 70s]) and sometimes discloses a sponsor (V6 at 25 s) or a bias (Google owns YouTube [V8 15s]).
- **Promise shown:** median 5.5 s (range 0–1031 s). Seven of 10 show it within 8 s. V10 only reaches its title question at 1031 s [V10 1031s].
- **Promise paid off:** median 440 s across the 9 videos that pay it off, which is about 49% of runtime (range 4% [V3 72s] to 96% [V1 3610s]). V10 never fully pays it off: it gives only "Halen Gemini 4 Argon'dan ses yok."
- **Self-introduction:** 0 of 10. The channel name is never spoken in the first 30 s, and the narrator never appears on camera.

## Structure and re-hooks
- **Common beat sequence:** cold-open claim → stakes, often a number → promise or roadmap → plain definition of the core term → a chain of tests or cases, each opened by a pivot → twist or honesty caveat → verdict or recap → open question or prediction → "write in the comments" → short goodbye.
- **Re-hook cadence:** the shorter videos (8–15 min) bring new stakes every 10–60 s: 25–40 s [V2], 10–30 s early on [V4], 20–50 s [V7], 30–40 s [V8], 30–60 s [V9]. The long videos start at 25–120 s and slow to 2–5 minutes in the middle: [V1] about 2–4 min between 1100 and 2800 s, [V3] 3–5 min later on, and a list-like product-update section in [V10] from about 780 to 1700 s.
- **Re-hook phrasing** is spoken, not edited: "Ama bakın problem şu" [V2 297s], "Ancak problem şu" [V3 520s], "Ama şimdi dürüst olmak lazım" [V7 440s], "Buna da kalmıyor" [V10 156s], "Peki…?" questions [V9 495s].
- **Open loops:** the title question is held to the middle or the end. The video often closes on a loop it leaves open on purpose: Adobe's valuation [V9 756s], who wins the agent market [V3 1620s], the OpenAI spy case [V5 830s].
- **Sponsors:** only 2 of 10 have a paid read, and both are early: Abacus at [V6 25s], Hostinger at [V3 70s] and [V3 120s]. V1 names a past sponsor as an example and says that video is not sponsored [V1 2200s]. V5 promotes the channel's own earlier videos [V5 515s].
- **CTA:** 9 of 10 end by asking for comments. V6 instead asks viewers to try the sponsor's link [V6 860s]. Four videos promise a next video (V4, V7, V8, V9).

## How it teaches
- **Ideas per video:** median 6 (range 3 [V8] to 12 [V3]; 68 ideas in total).
- **Vehicles across all 68 ideas:** example 17, definition 17, demo 11, analogy 9, misconception 7, question 4, story 2, visual 1.
- **Usual order:** claim or problem → plain-language definition → analogy or concrete example → evidence from his own test, a named case or a number → verdict. When an analogy is used for a hard term, it comes **before** the formal word. Examples:
  - The composer/score analogy appears in two videos for the same idea: "Bir program önce kaynak kodu olarak yazılıyor. Aynı bestecinin nota yazması gibi." [V7 35s]; "Besteci'nin notası gibi düşünebilirsiniz." [V9 80s].
  - Microscope for Ghidra [V7 80s].
  - Button and cable for abliteration [V2 194s].
  - Car repair for "AI is a tool" [V1 160s].
  - Employee or Jarvis for an agent [V3 1470s].
- **Code is not explained line by line** in any of the 10 descriptions. Technical ideas are carried by screen demos and results: the IINA plugin [V4 451s], the lemon button and the API-key guard [V7 495s], side-by-side model outputs [V8].
- **Jargon:** the narrator usually defines one core term per video and leaves the rest in English, assuming viewers know them. Examples: "REA" is never expanded [V2]; harness, distillation and compaction are not defined [V5]; MCP, fallback and token are not defined [V4]. Prior knowledge is often the previous video in the series [V4], [V7].
- **Misconceptions:** all 10 name and correct at least one, usually with a "değil" construction: "Bunlar modelin reddetmesi değil." [V2 122s], "Bir şeyi bir kere görmekle onu ezberlemek aynı şey değil." [V7 462s], "yapay zekanın aslında burada kötü bir niyeti yok" [V10 375s].
- **"Think first" moments:** one explicit timed pause: "Bakın 3 saniye veriyorum. Düşünün." [V1 1520s]. Elsewhere it is a rhetorical question answered within seconds: "Claude'a Ghidra'yı kurdurmaktan farkı ne?" [V7 90s], "Ne yapıyorlar normalde?" [V5 590s].
- **The hardest part:** handled with one analogy plus a memory instruction ("Bu ikincisini aklınızda tutun" [V9 95s]), with deliberately inflated numbers that he then admits to [V1 1670s], or by admitting his limits and handing the detail off ("matematik bilgim bunları anlamaya yetmiyor" [V10 561s]).
- **Recaps:** 7 of 10 have a clear closing summary. Three use the same phrase "Toparlamak gerekirse" [V1 3610s], [V7 690s], [V9 703s]. V2 returns to its three questions [V2 467s]. V3, V4 and V5 give only a short or informal close.
- **Story or real case:** the narrator's own test carries the teaching in 6 of 10 (V2, V4, V6, V7, V8, V9). V3 uses a recurring real user, his mother [V3 620s]. V1 uses a friend's shop and a viral ad. The roundups (V5, V10) run on a series of small incidents.

## Script language
**Tone:** informal, fast, friendly Turkish, like a peer talking through a lab session. He uses "arkadaşlar", "abi" (5 of 10: V1, V3, V5, V6, V8), "ya" and "yani". Viewers are mostly addressed with plural "siz". He switches to "sen" for accusations and hypotheticals ("Sen normalde aşırı teknik bir insan değilsin." [V6 50s]); V1 uses "sen" throughout. There are many imperatives: "kullanmayın" [V3], [V4], [V6], "Düşünün" [V1], [V9], "Unutmayın" [V6 770s].

**Sentence shape:** 5–6 words per sentence in all 10 videos. A claim is made, then split into two or three short sentences, often with the key word repeated:
- "Elimizde program var ama kodu yok. Sadece çalışan hali var." [V2 35s]
- "Yapay zeka bir sihir değil. Yapay zeka bir cin değil." [V1 138s]
- "Bakın bu hafta yaklaşık 10 farklı olay duyduk. 10 farklı." [V10 55s]

**Signposting:**
- "Bakın" before a reveal or result appears in 9 of 10. V7 uses "Peki şimdi ana soru şu." instead.
- "Şimdi", "Peki…?", "Yani" act as bridges.
- Numbered lists ("Bir, … İki, …") appear in 4 of 10 (V1, V2, V4, V9).
- Closure markers include "Bu kadar." and "Bitti." [V1], [V3].
- V8 has its own tic, "Onu da söyleyeyim", before each verdict.
- Tag or check questions ("değil mi?", "Tamam mı?", "Görüyor musunuz?", "anlıyor musunuz?") keep the viewer agreeing.

**Confidence language:** the verdict is firm, the frame is personal. A blunt line ("Burada Gemini Argon hile yaptı." [V8 277s], "Bence hiç girmeyin zaten." [V9 553s]) often sits next to a hedge: "bence", "gibi duruyor", "benim gördüğüm kadarıyla", "büyük ihtimalle". V1 is the least hedged. Evidence comes in three forms:
- **Counts and prices:** 2 vs 370 credits [V6 105s], 48 of 48 pixels [V9 45s], 3400 vs 353k tokens [V7 120s].
- **His own tests**, including their flaws, which builds trust: "Ama şimdi dürüst olmak lazım. 5'te 5 deyip geçersek biraz yalan olmuş olur." [V7 440s] and "Ajan benim hakemin hatasını buldu." [V9 636s].
- **Disclaimers:** bias [V8 15s], rumours ("Bu kesinleşmiş bir şey değil." [V10]), legal ("Yasal tavsiye veremem" [V7]).

**Humour and naturalness:** dry, self-mocking asides rather than set-up jokes: "Benim Sonnet 4 ile ne işim olabilir abi? Kapat." [V3], "Abi mükemmel yani. Beni şaşırttı." [V8]. The speech is clearly unpolished, with fillers and run-ons. The mascot's grumpy face does the visual comedy.

**Repetition and emphasis:** he repeats a thesis line as both a title card and speech ("Yani artık yavaş olan biziz." [V5 45s]), doubles words ("Neredeyse bedava." [V10 773s]) and restates the rule near the end ("Değerinizi kodun etrafında kurun." [V9 728s]).

**Opening and closing lines** follow a formula. Openings are a claim with no greeting. Closings are a one-line thesis, then "yorumlara yazın", then "Umarım beğenmişsinizdir. Hoşçakalın." [V7 805s].

## Limits
- **Sample:** one channel, its 10 latest long-form uploads as of 2026-11-03. The results may not hold for its older videos or for other channels.
- **Measured vs described:** cuts, visual changes, words per minute, first-speech time and sentence length were measured by code. Beats, techniques, ideas, vehicles and quotes were described by a model from transcripts, thumbnails and sampled frames, so the counts of techniques and vehicles reflect how that model labelled them. Transcript quotes come from speech recognition and may contain small errors.
- **No retention data:** YouTube does not publish audience retention, so no claim here links a technique to how long people actually watched. View counts are shown but were not used to rank techniques.
