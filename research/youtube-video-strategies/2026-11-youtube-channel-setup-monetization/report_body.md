# Growing a new long-form educational YouTube channel for an international English-speaking audience: what 30 videos support

## Short answer
The evidence is strong on a few points and thin on most of the rest. Four things are well supported. First, ad income depends mostly on **where viewers are**: US, UK, Canada, Australia and Western Europe pay far more per view than lower-income markets [K4]. Second, it depends on **format**: long-form earns tens of times more per view than Shorts [K15][K31]. Third, it depends on **topic** [K5]. Fourth, the YPP thresholds are confirmed by YouTube's docs: 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 public long-form watch hours or 10M Shorts views. They apply until the update on 2027-02-01 [K3][K176]. Studio setup advice also matches the docs: verify the account, set "not made for kids" for general-audience content, use upload defaults, and keep the notify-subscribers box ticked [K12][K37][K41][K142][K45]. These settings unlock features. None of them is shown to increase reach.

The corpus is weak on several parts of the question: SEO, tags, chapters, hashtags, translated titles, auto-dubbing results, end screens, playlists, publishing schedule and AI rules. Most of those claims are single-source or a restatement of the docs. Tags in particular play a minimal role according to YouTube itself [K44]. Income figures are anecdotes from creators who often sell courses, so do not use them as forecasts [K6][K8][K9].

## Levers
Ranked by expected impact on a new English-language educational channel that wants international reach and income.

1. **Make long-form videos of 8 minutes or more as the core output.** Long-form watch hours are the only ones that count toward the 4,000-hour threshold [K176]. Videos of 8 minutes or more get mid-roll ads with manual placement [K30][K195]. Long-form pays far more per view than Shorts [K15][K31]. Evidence: strong for the mechanics. One creator saw $750 more on a 9-minute video despite fewer views, but that is a single source [K40].
2. **Choose topics that attract advertisers and high-RPM audiences.** RPM depends on viewer country [K4] and on topic [K5][K197]. One creator saw a US CPM above $20 against under $1 RPM for India views [K192, single source]. Ranking whole niches (finance, business and AI above others) is **contested** [K32]. Evidence: strong for country and per-video variation, medium for niche ranking.
3. **Packaging and the opening.** Restate the title and thumbnail promise in the first seconds [K14]. Don't mislead: maliciously misleading titles and thumbnails break the spam policy [K38]. Get thumbnail ideas from top videos in your niche without copying them [K33][K26]. Use Test & Compare with up to 3 thumbnails; the winner is picked by watch-time share [K213]. Evidence: well supported as practice, though none of these sources show data on outcomes.
4. **Evergreen back catalogue.** Older videos can keep getting recommended while the topic stays interesting [K21]. Evidence: docs-backed, no figures.
5. **Revenue beyond ads.** Affiliate links [K233], sponsors and your own offer [K8][K128] may earn more than AdSense on a small channel. Evidence: anecdotal and from conflicted sources, so test it.
6. **Shorts for discovery.** Shorts reach new viewers but convert poorly to long-form and pay little [K19][K15]. Evidence: medium.
7. **Reach beyond English speakers: captions, clear speech, auto-dubbing.** Captions and clear, moderate-paced speech help international viewers [K25]. Auto-dubbing produces AI audio tracks and is on by default for eligible channels [K216][K217]. Effects on reach are single-source: one claim of "double or triple" reach [K219], and one creator's small dub gains [K115][K93]. Evidence: weak for any effect.
8. **Hygiene settings.** Verification [K12], audience setting [K37], channel keywords [K35], hashtags [K106], tags [K142][K44], chapters [K22]. Their direct effect on growth is low or unproven.

## Practical recipe
1. **Verify the channel.** In Studio go to Settings > Channel > Feature eligibility. Verify by phone, which unlocks intermediate features. Then do ID or video verification, or build enough channel history, for advanced features. Some options are not offered to every account. Use desktop Studio [K12][K63].
2. **Set the audience to "No, not made for kids"** if your content is general education. "Made for kids" removes comments, end screens, personalized ads and notifications. Choose "Yes" only if the content really targets children [K37][K41].
3. **Set the channel country to where you actually live** and check that it is a YPP-eligible market. Memberships depend on your country of residence [K92][K172].
4. **Fill in channel keywords** with niche terms. The field is harmless, but there's no evidence it boosts reach [K35].
5. **Set upload defaults:** a few channel-wide tags (tags play a minimal role [K44]) and any standard description text. Defaults only apply to uploads made in the browser [K142].
6. **For each video:**
   - Keep "Publish to subscriptions feed and notify subscribers" ticked. Notifications are capped at about 3 per channel per 24 hours [K45].
   - Use 3–5 hashtags in the description. Up to 3 are shown by the title [K106].
   - Either write manual chapters (they override automatic ones) or leave auto chapters on [K22].
   - Add captions [K25].
   - Use Test & Compare for thumbnails [K213].
7. **Aim for 8 minutes or more** where the content supports it, so mid-rolls are possible [K30][K195].
8. **Open by restating the title and thumbnail promise.** Keep the packaging truthful, or you risk the spam policy [K14][K38].
9. **Use Shorts as discovery**, but don't count on them for watch hours or revenue [K19][K176][K15].
10. **Track your YPP progress weekly.** The ads tier needs 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 public long-form hours in 12 months [K3]. The 500 subscribers / 3,000 hours tier, with 3 uploads in 90 days, unlocks fan funding only [K177]. Review takes about a month [K1]. Payout happens once you pass the threshold, about $100 [K181]. Check the rules again after 2027-02-01 [K3].
11. **If you use AI, add substantial original human work** (script, voice, editing). Mass-produced or templated AI content can be refused monetization [K24].
12. **Avoid sensitive framing that triggers age restriction**, for example on eating disorders. Age restriction cuts reach and ads [K102][K103].
13. **Never use self-engagement or second accounts** [K16].

## How to proceed: validate with your own data
1. **Check your geography and RPM (weeks 0–4, no cost).** Go to Studio > Revenue and break RPM down by country and by video over 28–90 days [K10 test][K31 test]. Decision: if US/UK/CA/AU views pay several times more than your other countries, choose future topics for those audiences.
2. **Check niche vs geography (90 days, about 10 or more videos).** Group videos by topic and by the share of viewers from high-RPM countries [K32 test][K196 test]. Decision: invest in a topic only if it still beats the others once country mix is accounted for.
3. **Shorts vs long-form subscribers (4 weeks, 5–10 Shorts cut from your long videos)** [K19 test]. Metric: subscribers per Short vs per long video, and whether long-form watch time rises. Decision: keep Shorts only if long-form watch time rises.
4. **Title strength (2 comparable videos, 7 days)** [K38 test]. Keep the stronger, still truthful title only if retention at 30 seconds doesn't drop noticeably.
5. **Tags (2 videos, 28 days)** [K44 test]. Expect no difference. If there is none, stop spending time on tags.
6. **Location tag (6 videos, 28 days)** [K27 test]. Decision: drop it unless the share of views from the target country rises consistently.
7. **Holding a video private before publishing (6–8 uploads)** [K20 test]. Alternate private-first and direct publishing, and keep the hold only if first-48-hour impressions and CTR improve consistently.
8. **One own offer or link (30–60 days, top 3–5 videos)** [K127 test][K8 test]. Keep it if conversions reach about 1% or more and revenue exceeds cost.
9. **Auto-dubbing on older videos (2–4 weeks)** [K218 test]. Then check dubbed-language views in your geography report against the single-source claim of "2–3x reach" [K219].

## Contested and conditional points
- **Does niche rank RPM?** Contested [K32]. Unverified tier tables [K10] don't match commenters' numbers. Geography and season may explain more.
- **"Small sample first" distribution of new videos:** contested and not in the docs [K13]. The idea of a fixed testing period or "your first video judges the channel" is doubtful [K11].
- **Engagement signals:** likes and watch behaviour are documented. Comment replies and same-channel follow-up views as ranking factors are not [K23]. "The algorithm's only goal is time on platform" is contradicted by the docs [K77].
- **Small channels get no penalty:** the matching goal is documented, but "no size penalty" is only an inference [K84].
- **Auto-translated captions:** plausible, but the "48 languages" figure is unverified [K34].
- **Location tag steering recommendations:** the two sources disagree, and the docs describe the tag only as a search and browse feature [K27].
- **Time to monetization:** ranges from 10 days to more than 6 months. Survivorship bias among creators who sell courses inflates the fast stories [K1][K139].
- **Seasonality:** real, but the size of peaks varies by niche and region [K105][K55][K104].

## Platform facts
- YPP ads tier: 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 public watch hours (12 months) or 10M Shorts views (90 days). Applies until 2027-02-01 [K3]. Shorts watch hours don't count toward the 4,000 [K176]. Fan-funding tier: 500 subscribers, 3,000 hours, 3 uploads in 90 days [K177].
- Long-form watch-page ads: creator gets 55% of net revenue. Shorts: creator gets 45% of their share of the pooled Creator Pool [K28][K31].
- CPM is the advertiser cost per 1,000 ad impressions. RPM is creator revenue per 1,000 views, after YouTube's share [K18]. Revenue depends on views and ads served, not on subscriber count [K187][K188].
- Mid-rolls require 8 minutes or more, with manual placement available [K30][K195].
- Payment threshold is about $100 and varies by currency [K181].
- Feature eligibility has three tiers: standard, intermediate, advanced [K12][K63].
- "Made for kids" removes comments, end screens, personalized ads and notifications [K41].
- Auto-dubbing: on by default for eligible channels. It excludes videos over 120 minutes and videos with little speech [K216][K217]. **Contradicted by docs:** "future uploads only". YouTube may also dub older videos [K218].
- Thumbnail Test & Compare: up to 3 thumbnails, winner chosen by watch-time share, long-form only [K213].
- Description hashtags: up to 3 shown by the title; more than 60 are ignored [K106].
- Manual chapters override automatic ones [K22].
- Tags play a minimal role. **The docs contradict** the claim that tags are the main signal for new channels [K44].
- AI policy: mass-produced, templated or generic content is excluded from monetization. Using AI as a tool is allowed [K24].
- Private videos are visible only to invited people [K61]. Premieres create a public landing page and reminders [K80]. Suggested videos is a separate traffic source [K72]. CTR and AVD definitions [K65][K94].
- **Contradicted by docs:** self-engagement [K16], using a false channel country [K172].

## Single-source but notable (unconfirmed)
- A 9-minute video with 30,000 fewer views earned $750 more than an under-8-minute video [K40].
- US views above $20 CPM (about $12–14 RPM) vs under $1 RPM for India on the same video [K192].
- Outlier videos bring subscribers who then watch the back catalogue, raising the daily view floor [K239].
- Link new long-form videos from existing Shorts via Studio > Related [K109].
- Captions helped one creator gain US, CA and AU viewers [K167]. Another creator's top countries shifted to the US, UK and CA after location and settings changes, but commenters disagree [K166].
- Auto-dub voice is sometimes robotic on technical passages [K75]. In one poll, 57% of subscribers didn't know auto-dubbing existed and 14% disliked it [K76]. Dubbed audiences watched longer than US English viewers on one channel [K93][K175].
- Low ad earnings in months with no uploads [K199]. A video every two weeks worked for one creator in a slow period [K203].
- A calendar booking link in the description brought one coaching client within a month [K126].
- One 2025 creator: $43,244 from long-form vs $5,594 from Shorts [K205].
- One creator: Amazon affiliate commission about 2.5% on tech items [K231]. For another educational channel: a few cents per item [K238].
- Choose dub languages from your top non-English geographies [K165].

## From the comments (unverified)
- Phone verification unlocks uploads over 15 minutes. Do it before planning long-form.
- Payouts can sit below $100 for months. The AdSense PIN step may start at about $10. Start payment and identity setup early.
- Meeting the thresholds doesn't switch on monetization automatically. Check the application and toggle status.
- Don't upload through a VPN to look foreign. One such channel stuck at about 17 views.
- Keep your channel country and bio truthful for tax reasons, and ask a tax advisor about payout arrangements.
- Track Shorts and long-form separately. Keep a weekly log of views and subscribers beyond Studio's 28-day window.
- Use a mixed strategy (Shorts for discovery, long-form for revenue). Commenters say it beat Shorts-only.
- Commit to a fixed cadence, such as weekly, for 4–6 months before judging. One commenter warns a long break can cost audience or monetization.
- RPM rises into Black Friday and Christmas.
- Made-for-kids content can have very low RPM (one report: about $0.50).

## Filtered out: sales, hype, unverifiable
- **Offers seen:** paid YouTube courses and "academies", paid communities (Telegram, membership clubs), coaching and paid channel audits, editing and agency services, affiliate links for growth tools (analytics software discounts, AI Shorts-clipping tools), music libraries, gear, and sponsored reads (drinks, watches, headphones, print-on-demand). Many of the most confident growth and income claims come from these sellers. None are linked here.
- **Hype:** one video's title and description promised an earnings comparison, but its transcript contains no content.
- **Unverifiable numbers:** "90% of creators quit within 20 videos" [K132], "76% use dark mode" [K133], "end reward adds about 50% watch time" [K71], "add 'AI' to titles to double RPM" [K46], file names help SEO [K210], phone verification routes early views by country [K155], new accounts are spam-limited [K43], same-day multi-upload dilution [K17], sponsors arrive at about 1,000 views per video [K179], $300–1,000 a month under 10k subscribers [K180], 2–3 years to real income [K139]. All personal income totals [K6][K9][K183]–[K209] are anecdotes, not benchmarks.

## Limits
- The sample is 30 videos from 27 channels. Several are Turkish, Indian or Nigerian creators, and many speakers sell courses or tools, so expect selection and survivorship bias.
- Coverage is thin for end screens, playlists, translated titles and descriptions, multi-language audio uploads and the details of AI-voice disclosure. This report can't give well-supported guidance on those.
- RPM and income figures are 2022–2026, in mixed currencies, and only valid for their own niche, audience and period.
- The YPP thresholds change from 2027-02-01. Check the official page.
- This reflects the videos available on 2026-11-03. Re-run it in 3–6 months, especially after the YPP update.
