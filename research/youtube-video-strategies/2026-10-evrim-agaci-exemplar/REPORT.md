# Evrim Ağacı: question titles, slow talking-head delivery and lessons built around myths

## Short answer
All 15 titles are plain questions ending in "?". In 14 of them the thumbnail adds a louder, all-caps claim or challenge, usually with the bearded presenter making a big face. None of the 15 videos opens with the thumbnail image. Each one starts with speech in the first half-second, either a bold claim, a question or a scenario aimed at "siz" (you). Then a calm, single-camera talking head speaks at about 119 words per minute. Viewers are held by a run of new questions, twists and "but here's the catch" turns, roughly every 1–2 minutes. The direct answer to the title or thumbnail usually comes late: the recorded median is 654 s. Teaching follows a stable pattern. A common belief is named and corrected, then an everyday analogy is given, then the term (often English plus a Turkish gloss), then concrete numbers or a real case. The pattern holds across physics, AI, biology and politics. The only real outlier is the event vlog [V5](https://youtu.be/HrwunGUvmbI).

The numbers show a channel whose pace comes from the voice, not the edit. No video cuts in its first 3 seconds, and the median video cuts only once in the first 10. The picture still changes about every 1.9 s, through B-roll, archival clips, diagrams and the host's constant gestures, so the screen rarely sits still even with few cuts. Speech starts at once and runs slightly faster in the first 10 s (132 wpm) than overall (119 wpm). In other words, the openings are tightly scripted and then settle into a lecture pace. The two extremes have clear causes: [V5](https://youtu.be/HrwunGUvmbI) is a travel vlog with long wordless event footage, and [V10](https://youtu.be/eIGgzub45e4) hands its middle over to long animated diagrams (12 s median shot).

- **The thumbnail is more provocative than the title.** The title asks; the thumbnail asserts a lie, a solved problem, a signal or a threat. - **Composition.** A bearded man, styled like the host, appears in 14 of 15 thumbnails. In [V11](https://youtu.be/upnh30GhRO4) the face is a suited politician-type figure who is not the host. Expressions are exaggerated: wide eyes or open mouth, grimacing, skeptical or tense. - **The curiosity gap** is nearly always "is the surprising claim true, and how?" Is the signal alien [V4](https://youtu.be/VMA_sSrjWLk)? ## Structure and re-hooks
**Common beat sequence** (seen in most of the 15): cold-open hook → promise or core question → setup of the "obvious" view → explanation blocks, each opened by a question → mid-video twist that reverses the obvious view → escalating "problems" or cases → payoff → widening stakes ("why does this matter to us?") → short closing reflection → link to another channel video.

**Re-hook cadence.** 13 of 15 records describe new hooks roughly every 60–120 s. [V10](https://youtu.be/eIGgzub45e4) is faster (about 40–60 s). [V9](https://youtu.be/AvAoQiZV0qg) and [V11](https://youtu.be/upnh30GhRO4) stretch to 1–3 min. The recurring re-hook types are:

## How it teaches
**Amount.** The median is **10 concepts per video** (range 5 [V12](https://youtu.be/mRfXV-BLoBI) – 15 [V6](https://youtu.be/BeEgfu_NJMs)), across 159 tagged concepts.

**Vehicles** (tags across all 159 concepts): definition 49, analogy 33, example 28, story/real case 18, misconception 15, visual 9, demo 4, question 3. 14 of 15 videos carry at least one concept mainly by analogy; 11 of 15 use a story or real case; only 5 rely on a visual as the main vehicle [V2](https://youtu.be/sgDe-ruggGE)[V4](https://youtu.be/VMA_sSrjWLk)[V8](https://youtu.be/YNGrMYo3aZw)[V10](https://youtu.be/eIGgzub45e4)[V14](https://youtu.be/NjnHbYtTZDc). The teaching is spoken first and shown second.

**Usual order of moves:** question or common belief → named as wrong → everyday analogy or thought experiment → the term (often English, then "yani" + Turkish) → numbers → real case or consequence. Typical chains:
- **Buoyancy [V6 1:22](https://youtu.be/BeEgfu_NJMs?t=82):** "how does a steel ship float?" → iceberg → displaced-water rule → melt the ship into a cube → 150,000-tonne tanker numbers.
- **Chirality [V3 15:50](https://youtu.be/REo8cLQolRo?t=950):** mirror hands → enantiomer definition → the lab-vs-life puzzle → 50.01 vs 49.99 imbalance [V3 19:25](https://youtu.be/REo8cLQolRo?t=1165) → the thalidomide case [V3 21:02](https://youtu.be/REo8cLQolRo?t=1262).
- **Quantisation [V14 6:47](https://youtu.be/NjnHbYtTZDc?t=407):** the VRAM problem → $350k cost → "kuantizasyon" named → 2-bit model on four RTX 3090s for $5–6k.

**Analogies are everyday and physical.** Honey vs water and an ice skater [V9](https://youtu.be/AvAoQiZV0qg); a hair's width and a finger held at arm's length [V4](https://youtu.be/VMA_sSrjWLk); a pipe in an ocean and a firefly beside a streetlamp [V7](https://youtu.be/qehnCSTeJqU); a passing ambulance and shouting while running [V8](https://youtu.be/YNGrMYo3aZw); teacher and student for distillation [V14](https://youtu.be/NjnHbYtTZDc); a short circuit for evolutionary misfiring [V12](https://youtu.be/mRfXV-BLoBI); a 4×4×4 m room for space dust [V2 14:00](https://youtu.be/sgDe-ruggGE?t=840).

**Jargon and prior knowledge.** Terms are glossed inline instead of being avoided. Examples: "Recursive Self Improvement veya kısaca RSI, Türkçesiyle yinelemeli öz geliştirme" [V1](https://youtu.be/Ec9jyLqYpxg); "singularity yani tekillik veya blow up yani patlama" [V9](https://youtu.be/AvAoQiZV0qg); "entrainment yani sürüklenme" [V8 16:45](https://youtu.be/YNGrMYo3aZw?t=1005). 7 of 15 lean on earlier channel videos as assumed knowledge or point to them for formulas [V2](https://youtu.be/sgDe-ruggGE)[V3](https://youtu.be/REo8cLQolRo)[V7](https://youtu.be/qehnCSTeJqU)[V8](https://youtu.be/YNGrMYo3aZw)[V10](https://youtu.be/eIGgzub45e4)[V13](https://youtu.be/jEUVhAvqgmA)[V14](https://youtu.be/NjnHbYtTZDc). **Recaps.** 9 of 15 have a closing beat labelled recap [V3](https://youtu.be/REo8cLQolRo)[V4](https://youtu.be/VMA_sSrjWLk)[V6](https://youtu.be/BeEgfu_NJMs)[V8](https://youtu.be/YNGrMYo3aZw)[V9](https://youtu.be/AvAoQiZV0qg)[V10](https://youtu.be/eIGgzub45e4)[V11](https://youtu.be/upnh30GhRO4)[V12](https://youtu.be/mRfXV-BLoBI)[V13](https://youtu.be/jEUVhAvqgmA). **Story or real case carrying the lesson.** In about half the videos a real thread carries the teaching:

## Script language
**Tone.** Conversational lecture: calm, warm and often dry, with bursts of mock drama ("Heyhat!", "Evet ciddiyim"). The host speaks to the viewer as "siz" throughout.

**Techniques:**
- Tag questions: "Öyle değil mi?", "değil mi?".
- Signpost pivots before each turn: "Peki…", "Ama…", "Bakın", "Dikkat edin", "Şöyle düşünün".
- "Diyelim ki" (let's say) scenarios.
- Ordinal lists: "İlki… İkincisi… Üçüncüsü".
- Short punch lines after long chained sentences. The median sentence is 13 words; key verdicts are often one to five words ("Cevap entropi." [V13](https://youtu.be/jEUVhAvqgmA); "Hayır. Tabii ki hayır." [V2 19:20](https://youtu.be/sgDe-ruggGE?t=1160)).

**Verbatim examples:**
- "Hatta ben bu videoda bunun tam tersini iddia edeceğim." [V2 1:15](https://youtu.be/sgDe-ruggGE?t=75)
- "Peki diyelim ki korkulan oldu ve geminiz batıyor. Ne yapacaksınız?" [V6 10:08](https://youtu.be/BeEgfu_NJMs?t=608)
- "Durun daha da yakacağım beyninizi." [V13 0:50](https://youtu.be/jEUVhAvqgmA?t=50)

## Limits
- **Sample.** One channel and its latest 15 long-form uploads, reflecting the channel as of 2026-11-03. The patterns may not hold for older videos, Shorts or other channels.
- **What code measured.** Cut counts, shot lengths, visual-change rates, speech rate and first-speech time were measured by code and are reliable.
- **What a model described.** Beats, re-hooks, promises, payoff times, concept lists, vehicle tags and quotes were described by a model from transcripts, thumbnails and sampled frames. Their timestamps are approximate; [V4](https://youtu.be/VMA_sSrjWLk)'s record says about ±10 s. A few fields contradict their own beat lists (the early payoff times for [V3](https://youtu.be/REo8cLQolRo), [V11](https://youtu.be/upnh30GhRO4) and [V13](https://youtu.be/jEUVhAvqgmA)). Transcripts contain speech-recognition errors.
- **No retention data.** YouTube does not publish audience retention, so "holds viewers" here means the techniques used, not proven effects. View counts (about 531k–1.63M) are not normalised for video age.
