# Evrim Ağacı: question titles, slow talking-head delivery and lessons built around myths

## Short answer
All 15 titles are plain questions ending in "?". In 14 of them the thumbnail adds a louder, all-caps claim or challenge, usually with the bearded presenter making a big face. None of the 15 videos opens with the thumbnail image. Each one starts with speech in the first half-second, either a bold claim, a question or a scenario aimed at "siz" (you). Then a calm, single-camera talking head speaks at about 119 words per minute. Viewers are held by a run of new questions, twists and "but here's the catch" turns, roughly every 1–2 minutes. The direct answer to the title or thumbnail usually comes late: the recorded median is 654 s. Teaching follows a stable pattern. A common belief is named and corrected, then an everyday analogy is given, then the term (often English plus a Turkish gloss), then concrete numbers or a real case. The pattern holds across physics, AI, biology and politics. The only real outlier is the event vlog [V5].

The numbers show a channel whose pace comes from the voice, not the edit. No video cuts in its first 3 seconds, and the median video cuts only once in the first 10. The picture still changes about every 1.9 s, through B-roll, archival clips, diagrams and the host's constant gestures, so the screen rarely sits still even with few cuts. Speech starts at once and runs slightly faster in the first 10 s (132 wpm) than overall (119 wpm). In other words, the openings are tightly scripted and then settle into a lecture pace. The two extremes have clear causes: [V5] is a travel vlog with long wordless event footage, and [V10] hands its middle over to long animated diagrams (12 s median shot).

## Packaging (title + thumbnail)
- **The title is a neutral, curious question: 15 of 15.** Examples: "Batan Gemide Nasıl Hayatta Kalırsın?" [V6], "Zaman Hakkında Ne Biliyoruz?" [V13], "Jet Motorları Neden Bu Kadar Gürültülü?" [V8].
- **The thumbnail text is a second, louder line, not the title: 14 of 15.** Only [V6] repeats the title on the thumbnail. All 15 use all-caps text. 8 end in "!" ("IŞIK HIZI yalanı!" [V2], "UZAYDAN SİNYAL ALDIK!" [V4], "MİLYONLUK SORU ÇÖZÜLDÜ!" [V9], "ZAMAN GERÇEK OLMAYABİLİR!" [V13], "EBEDİ MUHALEFET!" [V11]). 6 are blunt questions aimed at the viewer ("SEN NE İŞ YAPACAKSIN?" [V1], "GERİ Mİ KALDIN?" [V14], "EINSTEIN NE ZIRVALIYOR?" [V10]). 1 is a statement [V15].
- **The thumbnail is more provocative than the title.** The title asks; the thumbnail asserts a lie, a solved problem, a signal or a threat. In [V2] "light speed is a lie" sits on top of "can we reach anywhere instantly?". In [V9] "the million-dollar question is solved!" sits on top of "did OpenAI solve it or steal it?". In [V3] the thumbnail ("KİM KAZANDI?", a Nobel medal) does not match the brain-control title at all.
- **Composition.** A bearded man, styled like the host, appears in 14 of 15 thumbnails. In [V11] the face is a suited politician-type figure who is not the host. Expressions are exaggerated: wide eyes or open mouth, grimacing, skeptical or tense. In 6 he is in costume or role-play: robot-armed beggar [V1], spacecraft cockpit [V2], straw hat and headphones [V4], hi-vis vest beside an engine [V8], Einstein look-alike [V10][V15]. At least 8 of the descriptions mention a red/orange-to-yellow (or pink-red) gradient border.
- **The curiosity gap** is nearly always "is the surprising claim true, and how?" Is the signal alien [V4]? How can I be moving at light speed right now [V10]? Do animals keep pets, and does a tarantula really live with a frog [V12]?

## The first 3, 10 and 30 seconds
**Order of events.** Speech starts at 0–0.4 s in 15 of 15 videos. The host is on camera within the first 3 s in 13 of 15; 4 of those fade up from a black first frame [V3][V7][V10][V13]. The two exceptions open on a press-release webpage with a statistic [V12] and on colourised archival footage [V15]. The thumbnail image or setting is on screen at the start in **0 of 15**.

**Opening device (first sentence):**
- Bold claim or "news": 5 of 15. Examples: "Uzaydan çok ama çok özel bir sinyal aldık." [V4 0s], "Siz şu anda ışık hızında hareket ediyorsunuz." [V10 0s], plus [V9][V13][V14].
- Question to the viewer, or an objection to challenge: 4. Examples: "Ben size bu kanalda hep ne diyorum?" [V2 0s], "Siyasi partilerin amacını kazanmak, değil mi?" [V11 0s], plus [V5][V8].
- Real event or statistic: 2. The Filo Jet sinking [V6 0s]; a pet-ownership survey [V12 0s].
- Second-person hypothetical scene: 2 [V1 0s][V7 0s].
- List tease: 1 [V3 0s].
- Famous quote, then questioned: 1 [V15 0s].

**By 10 s** the premise is set in concrete terms: numbers like 8 minutes, 4 years and 2.5 million years [V2], the ferry's passenger count and wreck depth [V6], or a challenge to "what will you do?" [V1]. **By 30 s** the core question is usually stated and B-roll has started. Several videos close the opening with "Gelin öğrenelim" (let's find out) [V4][V15]. Two videos put a sponsor straight after the hook: Honor at 35 s [V4 35s] and Dyson at about 60 s [V8 60s].

**Promise timing.** The thumbnail or title promise is first stated at a median of **20 s** (range 0–154 s); 6 of 15 state it at 0 s. Late examples: [V7 154s], [V8 110s]. The payoff is recorded for 14 videos, at a median of **654 s** (about 11 min; range 15–1670 s). [V1] never pays off its job question concretely. Three records give a payoff time inside the first 30 s that their own beat lists contradict. [V3] says the topics are Nobel topics at 15 s, but names winners only from [V3 96s]. [V11] states the thesis early, but the answer comes at [V11 1080s]. [V13] lists a payoff at 27 s, but the real payoff is at [V13 1670s]. Using the beat-list times for those three, the median payoff is about 870 s. Either way, the answer lands mid-video or later. Late examples: [V9 1640s], [V14 1670s], [V15 1345s].

**Self-introduction.** None in 15 of 15. The host never says his name, and the channel name is at most mentioned in passing.

## Structure and re-hooks
**Common beat sequence** (seen in most of the 15): cold-open hook → promise or core question → setup of the "obvious" view → explanation blocks, each opened by a question → mid-video twist that reverses the obvious view → escalating "problems" or cases → payoff → widening stakes ("why does this matter to us?") → short closing reflection → link to another channel video.

**Re-hook cadence.** 13 of 15 records describe new hooks roughly every 60–120 s. [V10] is faster (about 40–60 s). [V9] and [V11] stretch to 1–3 min. The recurring re-hook types are:
- **A reversal of what was just said.** "Sakin çünkü burada bazı detayları atladık…" [V2 634s]; "türbülans her zaman bir düşman değil" [V8 866s]; "Ama bir opsiyon daha var" [V13 1631s].
- **A new, sharper number.** 1.6 TB of VRAM ≈ $350k [V14 485s]; 50 Hiroshima bombs from one dust grain [V2 960s]; 1250 Gauss vs Earth's 0.5 [V4 1120s].
- **The title question asked again** right before the answer. "Ne iş yapacaksınız da para kazanacaksınız?" [V1 18s]; "Peki bu kadar veri ne işimize yarayacak?" [V7 788s]; the alien question reopened [V4 855s].
- **A held-back teaser.** "size hala ideal senaryoyu söylemedim" [V6 1316s]; "Ha bir de bakın … kiminle karşılaştık" [V5 690s]; "Burayı aklınızda tutun birazdan önemli olacak" [V9].
- **A story turn.** The NRO spy-mirror call [V7 450s]; the OpenAI threat [V9 1230s].
- **"Why should you care?"** "Peki tüm bunlardan bize ne?" [V4 1228s]; "will this change our lives?" [V9 1676s].

**Open loops.** Each video opens several questions (records list 3–7). The title loop is the longest and usually closes late. Smaller loops close within 1–3 minutes, for example how to catch a ghost particle [V3 205s→268s]. Most videos leave at least one loop open for a future video: the warp-drive follow-up [V2 745s], the resolution of the twin paradox [V10 1040s], general relativity [V15 1497s].

**Sponsor and CTA placement.**
- **Paid sponsors** appear in 2 of 15, both in the first minute after the hook [V4 35s][V8 60s]. [V8] weaves the product into the lesson and returns to it at [V8 1092s].
- **Viewer-support pleas** (membership/Kreosus) appear in 4: early [V2 104s], mid-video [V14 1300s] and at the end [V13 1745s][V15 1579s].
- **A closing pointer to another channel video** appears in 15 of 15. Notification asks: [V2 755s][V10 1055s][V15 1579s]. Comment asks: [V1][V5][V14].

## How it teaches
**Amount.** The median is **10 concepts per video** (range 5 [V12] – 15 [V6]), across 159 tagged concepts.

**Vehicles** (tags across all 159 concepts): definition 49, analogy 33, example 28, story/real case 18, misconception 15, visual 9, demo 4, question 3. 14 of 15 videos carry at least one concept mainly by analogy; 11 of 15 use a story or real case; only 5 rely on a visual as the main vehicle [V2][V4][V8][V10][V14]. The teaching is spoken first and shown second.

**Usual order of moves:** question or common belief → named as wrong → everyday analogy or thought experiment → the term (often English, then "yani" + Turkish) → numbers → real case or consequence. Typical chains:
- **Buoyancy [V6 82s]:** "how does a steel ship float?" → iceberg → displaced-water rule → melt the ship into a cube → 150,000-tonne tanker numbers.
- **Chirality [V3 950s]:** mirror hands → enantiomer definition → the lab-vs-life puzzle → 50.01 vs 49.99 imbalance [V3 1165s] → the thalidomide case [V3 1262s].
- **Quantisation [V14 407s]:** the VRAM problem → $350k cost → "kuantizasyon" named → 2-bit model on four RTX 3090s for $5–6k.

**Analogies are everyday and physical.** Honey vs water and an ice skater [V9]; a hair's width and a finger held at arm's length [V4]; a pipe in an ocean and a firefly beside a streetlamp [V7]; a passing ambulance and shouting while running [V8]; teacher and student for distillation [V14]; a short circuit for evolutionary misfiring [V12]; a 4×4×4 m room for space dust [V2 840s].

**Jargon and prior knowledge.** Terms are glossed inline instead of being avoided. Examples: "Recursive Self Improvement veya kısaca RSI, Türkçesiyle yinelemeli öz geliştirme" [V1]; "singularity yani tekillik veya blow up yani patlama" [V9]; "entrainment yani sürüklenme" [V8 1005s]. 7 of 15 lean on earlier channel videos as assumed knowledge or point to them for formulas [V2][V3][V7][V8][V10][V13][V14]. [V10] is the most dependent: it builds directly on [V15]'s photon clock. Formulas are usually spoken or deferred rather than derived.

**Misconceptions** are the main engine. Every video corrects at least 3 (median 4). Examples:
- Einstein discovered constant light speed → it was Maxwell [V15 313s].
- Ships sink slowly like the Titanic → it can take minutes [V6 1012s].
- Open weights = open source [V14 350s].
- Apex predators are evolution's goal [V11 530s].
- The giant sinking-ship vortex [V6 1403s].

**"Think first" moments.** A median of 5 per video, where the viewer is asked to predict before the answer. Examples: "Bunların ortak noktası ne dersiniz?" [V3 12s]; "is this video playing forward or backward?" [V13 720s]; solve the payoff matrix yourself [V11 1010s]; "Ne yapacaksınız?" [V6 608s].

**The hardest part** gets one of three treatments:
- **One analogy repeated in new frames.** Entropy is taught through the egg, gas in a room, dominoes and coffee [V13]; the 2D shadow world gets a second shadow, then Pythagoras [V10 410s].
- **Slowing down and walking through step by step.** The payoff matrix is read column by column, with its numbers openly called made-up [V11]; the photon clock is run in two frames [V15 1230s].
- **Admitting the limit openly.** "Zaman ne? Bilmiyoruz." [V13 1711s]; the task model "cannot be fully unpacked here" [V1].

**Recaps.** 9 of 15 have a closing beat labelled recap [V3][V4][V6][V8][V9][V10][V11][V12][V13]. These tend to restate the big idea or re-ask the opening question rather than re-explain. 7 records say there is no formal recap.

**Story or real case carrying the lesson.** In about half the videos a real thread carries the teaching:
- a recent news event: the Filo Jet sinking [V6], a 2026 preprint solved like a detective story [V4];
- the history of a project: the Roman telescope [V7];
- a dispute: OpenAI vs Alpöge–Buckmaster [V9];
- a chain of numbered "vakas": the animal pet cases [V12];
- the presenter's own trip: [V5].

The rest are carried by thought experiments instead: a ship to the horizon [V2], Einstein's train and lamp [V15].

## Script language
**Tone.** Conversational lecture: calm, warm and often dry, with bursts of mock drama ("Heyhat!", "Evet ciddiyim"). The host speaks to the viewer as "siz" throughout.

**Techniques:**
- Tag questions: "Öyle değil mi?", "değil mi?".
- Signpost pivots before each turn: "Peki…", "Ama…", "Bakın", "Dikkat edin", "Şöyle düşünün".
- "Diyelim ki" (let's say) scenarios.
- Ordinal lists: "İlki… İkincisi… Üçüncüsü".
- Short punch lines after long chained sentences. The median sentence is 13 words; key verdicts are often one to five words ("Cevap entropi." [V13]; "Hayır. Tabii ki hayır." [V2 1160s]).

**Verbatim examples:**
- "Hatta ben bu videoda bunun tam tersini iddia edeceğim." [V2 75s]
- "Peki diyelim ki korkulan oldu ve geminiz batıyor. Ne yapacaksınız?" [V6 608s]
- "Durun daha da yakacağım beyninizi." [V13 50s]
- "Başta her şey iyi gidiyordu. Ta ki Trump başa gelene kadar." [V7 585s]
- "Bütün kuğular beyazdır iddiasını çürütmek için tek bir tanecik siyah kuğu bulmanız yeterli." [V9 1640s]
- "Tüm bunlar ışığında. Tekrar soralım. Zaman ne? Bilmiyoruz." [V13 1711s]

## Limits
- **Sample.** One channel and its latest 15 long-form uploads, reflecting the channel as of 2026-11-03. The patterns may not hold for older videos, Shorts or other channels.
- **What code measured.** Cut counts, shot lengths, visual-change rates, speech rate and first-speech time were measured by code and are reliable.
- **What a model described.** Beats, re-hooks, promises, payoff times, concept lists, vehicle tags and quotes were described by a model from transcripts, thumbnails and sampled frames. Their timestamps are approximate; [V4]'s record says about ±10 s. A few fields contradict their own beat lists (the early payoff times for [V3], [V11] and [V13]). Transcripts contain speech-recognition errors.
- **No retention data.** YouTube does not publish audience retention, so "holds viewers" here means the techniques used, not proven effects. View counts (about 531k–1.63M) are not normalised for video age.
