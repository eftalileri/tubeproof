# Kurzgesagt, late 2026: how the videos hold attention and teach

## Short answer
Kurzgesagt gets the click with a short, high-stakes title and a mostly dark thumbnail. The thumbnail shows one glowing subject and two or three words in white capitals that add a threat or a mystery the title leaves out ("95% DEATH RATE", "WE LOST CONTROL", "IT'S IMPOSSIBLE*"). Once you're watching, the video doesn't hold you with fast editing. It cuts only about 5.7 times a minute, but the picture keeps moving inside each shot. Speech starts within half a second of the opening, and the first line is usually a bold claim or a statement that something you believe is wrong. After that comes a new stake, a "But…" turn, or a question roughly every 60–90 seconds. The thumbnail's promise is usually paid off late (median about 4.5 minutes), so the gap it opens carries the viewer through the middle. Teaching follows a steady pattern: a question or false belief, then an analogy or a plain one-line definition, then a concrete number or case. The pattern is very consistent across the 12 standard explainers. V2 (a calendar pitch) and V15 (a channel-launch teaser) are short exceptions.

What the numbers mean:
- **Movement, not cuts.** The picture changes about 2.4 times as often as the editor cuts (13.5 changes vs 5.7 cuts a minute). Something new appears on screen every ~3 s, while a shot lasts ~6 s.
- **No silent intros.** Talking starts almost at once in every video.
- **A slow start that speeds up.** Speech in the first 10 s is slower than the video's average in 13 of 15 videos. V1 opens at 72 wpm and V14 at 90 wpm. Pace climbs to ~150–170 wpm once the explaining starts. The two exceptions start fast: V3 and V10 (V10 runs 180 wpm from the first second).
- **Two outliers.** V14 is a slow, ominous space tour (1.5 cuts a minute, ~20 s shots). V2 is a fast-cut pitch (12.4 cuts a minute).

## Packaging (title + thumbnail)
- **The thumbnail text adds to the title instead of repeating it: 14 of 15.** All carry text except V15, and 13 of the 14 use 2–3 words in white capitals. The text usually supplies the stake or the label the title leaves out:
  - a number: "95% DEATH RATE" [V1](https://youtu.be/5_Fayfx2NUM), "100% VIRUS" [V6](https://youtu.be/0NY2gAftzJE)
  - doom: "LOST FOREVER" [V3](https://youtu.be/_y9JuojAIg0), "WE LOST CONTROL" [V4](https://youtu.be/ujkD4SxPKOI), "IT'S IMPOSSIBLE*" [V7](https://youtu.be/Cyl3X88KEgg)
  - a named mystery: "OMEGA ALIENS" [V5](https://youtu.be/QW_jlUn4gA8), "TERRA NUKE" [V13](https://youtu.be/h3DCdWyb0cc), "SUPERVOIDS" + "Hello...?" [V14](https://youtu.be/yDAAlojz8NU)
  - Only V10 ("OZEMPIC / UPDATE!") and V11 ("Germany") largely repeat the title.
- **Composition: one glowing subject on a dark purple or navy background.** No human face appears in any thumbnail. Recurring helpers:
  - an arrow pointing at the subject: 5 videos (red in V5, V8, V9, V12; white in V6)
  - a small labelled "EARTH" for scale: 2 (V5, V9)
  - a three-step diagram whose third panel is blurred, which is a built-in curiosity gap: V13
- **The kind of promise:**
  - threat or doom: 6 (V1, V3, V4, V7, V11, V14)
  - wonder or "how does X work": 4 (V5, V8, V9, V12)
  - a medical story: V6
  - an update: V10
  - an odd piece of history: V13
  - channel or shop business: 2 (V2, V15)
- **Title wording:**
  - 9 use an extreme or absolute word: "Terrifying", "Insane", "Most Insane", "Scariest", "Godlike", "Sucks", "Uncomfortable Truth", "IS OVER", "Never"
  - 4 are How/Why titles (V1, V7, V8, V9)
  - 3 speak to "you" (V2, V12, V13: "You Never Heard About")
- **Curiosity gaps the video never closes.** Some thumbnail elements are never answered on screen: the "Hello...?" voice in V14, the term "terra nuke" (never said aloud in V13), and which "half" you won't like in V2.

## The first 3, 10 and 30 seconds
**First 3 s.** The line spoken is the hook:
- **A bold or contrarian claim: 8 of 15.**
  - "You probably think you know human history, but you don't." [V3 0:00](https://youtu.be/_y9JuojAIg0?t=0)
  - "Everything we think about aliens might be completely wrong" [V5 0:00](https://youtu.be/QW_jlUn4gA8?t=0)
  - "You will never leave the solar system." [V7 0:00](https://youtu.be/Cyl3X88KEgg?t=0)
  - also V8, V10, V11, V12, V14
- **Dropped into the middle of an event: 3.** A date-stamped AI premise [V4 0:00](https://youtu.be/ujkD4SxPKOI?t=0), a needle entering a tumour [V6 0:00](https://youtu.be/0NY2gAftzJE?t=0), "the US planned to nuke Israel" [V13 0:00](https://youtu.be/h3DCdWyb0cc?t=0).
- **A shouted order: 1.** "Hey, wake up!" [V2 0:00](https://youtu.be/PXbVn-f_wuM?t=0)
- **A promo or announcement first: 3.** A calendar ad [V1 0:00](https://youtu.be/5_Fayfx2NUM?t=0), a game promo [V9 0:00](https://youtu.be/8qQW4LTWgtc?t=0), the channel announcement [V15 0:00](https://youtu.be/D0WQK7cMJn8?t=0).

The opening picture is usually calm and sparse: a single figure, slow drift, no cut. It lets the sentence do the work.

**First 10 s.** The claim is widened with a scale or an analogy, for example "like dark matter" [V3 0:14](https://youtu.be/_y9JuojAIg0?t=14) or "not static like photos" [V8 0:09](https://youtu.be/PqtggjVAi8M?t=9).

**By 30 s.** The stakes are stated and the guiding question is set: "How is a moment in time stored in squishy meat?" [V8 0:40](https://youtu.be/PqtggjVAi8M?t=40), "Could there be a planet that's not just more suitable for life, but ideal?" [V9 0:25](https://youtu.be/8qQW4LTWgtc?t=25).

**Promise timing:**
- The opening picture matches the thumbnail in only 3 of 15 (V2, V3, V6).
- The thumbnail's promise is first stated in words at a median of 2 s. Six videos state it at 0 s; outliers are 40 s [V8](https://youtu.be/PqtggjVAi8M) and 240 s [V13](https://youtu.be/h3DCdWyb0cc).
- The recorded payoff comes at a median of 270 s (range 8–930 s). Three of the early "payoffs" (V6, V10, V11) mark where the promise is restated rather than resolved. Using their actual payoff beats (V6 ~680 s, V10 ~750 s, V11 ~624 s), the median is ~320 s.
- Late payoffs:
  - the first named person, Khushum [V3 15:15](https://youtu.be/_y9JuojAIg0?t=915)
  - the 700-agent cyberattack [V4 15:05](https://youtu.be/ujkD4SxPKOI?t=905)
  - "omega minus" [V5 12:28](https://youtu.be/QW_jlUn4gA8?t=748)
  - the species-count chart [V9 11:55](https://youtu.be/8qQW4LTWgtc?t=715)
- Early payoffs:
  - the barrier is named [V7 2:55](https://youtu.be/Cyl3X88KEgg?t=175), though its asterisk is only resolved in the closing hedge [V7 12:15](https://youtu.be/Cyl3X88KEgg?t=735)
  - "a memory is an activation pattern" [V8 5:20](https://youtu.be/PqtggjVAi8M?t=320)

**Self-introduction.** The creators never appear on camera, and 11 of 15 videos never introduce themselves. The exceptions:

## Structure and re-hooks
**Common beat sequence (standard explainers):**
1. Hook claim
2. Stakes and promise (0–30 s)
3. "First, let's set the stage" background, often 2–5 minutes
4. A ladder of escalating steps or a chronological story
5. Mid-roll sponsor
6. "And now, back to…" plus a new stake
7. Twists that build toward the payoff
8. A hedged verdict or reflection
9. Shop call to action
10. Soundtrack outro

**Re-hook cadence.** In 12 of 15 videos a new stake, twist or question arrives roughly every 60–90 s (30–120 s at the extremes). The short or promo videos are faster: V2 every 6–15 s early on, V15 every 8–12 s during the raid, and V1 every 20–40 s in its first five minutes. Typical re-hook lines:
- "But then, a short moment of inattention and disaster struck." [V1 1:05](https://youtu.be/5_Fayfx2NUM?t=65)
- "Things should have ended here." [V4 10:00](https://youtu.be/ujkD4SxPKOI?t=600)
- "Oh, things are even worse actually." [V4 16:30](https://youtu.be/ujkD4SxPKOI?t=990)
- "Update, it happened." [V4 17:40](https://youtu.be/ujkD4SxPKOI?t=1060)
- "Space hates you." [V7 5:06](https://youtu.be/Cyl3X88KEgg?t=306)
- "Unfortunately, it's a hot, dead desert." [V9 3:05](https://youtu.be/8qQW4LTWgtc?t=185)
- "The fiasco begins." [V13 5:50](https://youtu.be/h3DCdWyb0cc?t=350)

A weak spot shows up repeatedly: long background sections (V4 77–386 s, the mechanism in V1 330–565 s) lean on analogies rather than new plot turns.

**Ladders as the spine.** In 4 videos each new step is itself the re-hook:
- the "Barrow scale" levels, from control of DNA down to the "bottom of reality" [V5](https://youtu.be/QW_jlUn4gA8)
- the checklist for designing the ideal planet [V9](https://youtu.be/8qQW4LTWgtc)
- the species ladder: cell, roundworm, bee, octopus, jay, human [V12](https://youtu.be/dl0-TveDDGA)
- a guided tour outward from Earth into a supervoid [V14](https://youtu.be/yDAAlojz8NU)

**Open loops.** A median of 5 open loops per video. In 11 of 15 at least one is never closed, for example why the dead's heads were removed [V3 8:30](https://youtu.be/_y9JuojAIg0?t=510) and why benefits appear regardless of weight lost [V10 9:35](https://youtu.be/TYhNHX372ek?t=575).

**Sponsors and calls to action:**
- A third-party sponsor appears in 10 of 15. In 9 it sits mid-video: 8 start between 230 s and 357 s (median ~285 s), and V11's position isn't timestamped. V1 puts it near the end [V1 12:15](https://youtu.be/5_Fayfx2NUM?t=735). Ground News appears in 4 (V8, V10, V11, V13).
- Sponsor reads are often tied to the topic:
  - framed as answering a viewer's objection: "Hey, Mr. Kurzgesagt" [V5 5:30](https://youtu.be/QW_jlUn4gA8?t=330)
  - a VPN joke about online viruses in the virus video [V6 5:10](https://youtu.be/0NY2gAftzJE?t=310)
  - news bias in the Cold War propaganda video [V13 4:50](https://youtu.be/h3DCdWyb0cc?t=290)
- 11 of 15 end on the channel's own shop (calendar, prints, pins, figurines). V13 ends on a sister-channel plug.
- 11 of 15 close by pointing to an alternate audio track that plays the soundtrack.
- Self-promotion can also sit at the very start: the calendar ad in V1. V2 spends about 60% of its runtime on the calendar pitch [V2 1:21](https://youtu.be/PXbVn-f_wuM?t=81).

## How it teaches
**Volume.** A median of 8 ideas per video (range 6–13).

**Usual order of moves:** a question or false belief, then an analogy or one-line plain definition, then a concrete number or case, then a consequence or twist. Examples:
- **AI training, V4:** King Midas [V4 2:50](https://youtu.be/ujkD4SxPKOI?t=170) → the CoastRunners game [V4 3:20](https://youtu.be/ujkD4SxPKOI?t=200) → the term "reward hacking" defined [V4 4:50](https://youtu.be/ujkD4SxPKOI?t=290) → "But what if the task is actually impossible to solve?" [V4 5:05](https://youtu.be/ujkD4SxPKOI?t=305).
- **Pensions, V11:** a viewer's objection ("at least the young can… save money, right?") → the tax share → a short verdict.

Examples of analogies that recur across a whole video:

**Jargon.** Usually defined in one sentence right after the term: LLM and agent [V4 1:17](https://youtu.be/ujkD4SxPKOI?t=77), GLP-1 [V10 6:30](https://youtu.be/TYhNHX372ek?t=390), pay-as-you-go pensions [V11 2:20](https://youtu.be/n-gYFcVx-8Y?t=140), apoptosis as "Program cell death" [V1 8:40](https://youtu.be/5_Fayfx2NUM?t=520). Two videos assume more: V5 (DNA, the quantum vacuum) and V14 (dark energy). Huge numbers are converted to human scale:
- the Parker Solar Probe goes "around the world in four minutes" [V7 1:50](https://youtu.be/Cyl3X88KEgg?t=110)
- a grain of dust at speed "is like running into a grenade" [V7 6:20](https://youtu.be/Cyl3X88KEgg?t=380)
- "170 Hiroshimas" of explosive power [V13 3:40](https://youtu.be/h3DCdWyb0cc?t=220)

**Misconceptions and "think first" moments:**
- 14 of 15 correct a stated misconception, often in the first line. "But memories are not static like photos." [V8 0:09](https://youtu.be/PqtggjVAi8M?t=9)
- All 15 pose questions before giving the answer. "Why don't we just go faster?" [V7 3:20](https://youtu.be/Cyl3X88KEgg?t=200); "Okay, but what about immigration?" [V11 9:06](https://youtu.be/n-gYFcVx-8Y?t=546); "What was happening inside Beata's body? Let's go back in time." [V6 8:20](https://youtu.be/0NY2gAftzJE?t=500)

**The hardest part** is handled in four ways:
- a chain of analogies (V1, V8, V10, V15)
- an ordered "in a nutshell" list of steps [V13 4:10](https://youtu.be/h3DCdWyb0cc?t=250)
- small arithmetic steps, repeated (100 Germans → 24 descendants in four generations [V11](https://youtu.be/n-gYFcVx-8Y))
- explicit hedges: "We don't know" [V3 8:30](https://youtu.be/_y9JuojAIg0?t=510), "we can only speculate" [V5](https://youtu.be/QW_jlUn4gA8)

**Recaps.** Rare:
- an explicit recap section in 5 (V4, V6, V7, V10, V13)
- a one-line recap in V2
- a closing restatement of the thesis in 8
- nothing in V15

**What carries the teaching:**
- a real case or narrative: 6 (the creator's own cancer [V1](https://youtu.be/5_Fayfx2NUM), ancient sites up to the Khushum tablet [V3](https://youtu.be/_y9JuojAIg0), the AI sandbox breach [V4](https://youtu.be/ujkD4SxPKOI), Beata's self-treatment [V6](https://youtu.be/0NY2gAftzJE), Project Plowshare [V13](https://youtu.be/h3DCdWyb0cc), the 1809 raid [V15](https://youtu.be/D0WQK7cMJn8))
- a ladder, tour or build frame: 4 (V5, V9, V12, V14)
- a stacked argument: 4 (V7, V8, V10, V11)
- a sales pitch: 1 (V2)

## Script language
The tone is calm, curious and wry, with dry jokes and dark humour. The narrator speaks to "you" and "we" and is open about what is uncertain. Sentences are short to medium (median 14 words). Long sentences full of numbers are punctuated by two- to five-word stings ("Oh well.", "This was bad."). Turns are signposted with "But…", "Until…", "Unfortunately…" and "In a nutshell…". Reveals are often timed with an ellipsis and a pause. Example lines:

- "We reach it in... 2500 years. Oh. Oh no." [V7 2:32](https://youtu.be/Cyl3X88KEgg?t=152)
- "A village can live from memory. A city can't." [V3 14:45](https://youtu.be/_y9JuojAIg0?t=885)
- "Boom. It works." [V4 14:40](https://youtu.be/ujkD4SxPKOI?t=880)
- "Let's say that again. One in four German tax euros is used to pay out pensions today." [V11 3:30](https://youtu.be/n-gYFcVx-8Y?t=210)
- "Your hunger is hormones." [V10 5:30](https://youtu.be/TYhNHX372ek?t=330)
- "This is not a sad story." [V1 0:08](https://youtu.be/5_Fayfx2NUM?t=8)

## Limits
- **Sample.** One channel and its latest 15 uploads as of 2026-11-03. Two of them are a short pitch (V2) and a teaser (V15), and they skew some medians.
- **What was measured vs described.** Cuts, visual changes, speech rate and timing were measured by code. Beats, promises, payoff times, teaching vehicles and tone were described by a model from transcripts, thumbnails and sampled frames. Some of these seconds are estimates (V1, V15), and V11's beat times were logged in minutes; I converted them where I used them. The model-labelled payoff times for V6, V10 and V11 look like restated promises, as noted above.
- **No retention data.** YouTube does not publish retention, so nothing here shows which techniques actually keep viewers. View counts (69k to 22.7M) are not controlled for age or topic.
