# How Veritasium holds and teaches: 12 recent long-form videos

## Short answer
These videos get the click with a dark, faceless thumbnail and a few words of text. Ten of the 12 thumbnails carry a short phrase, either a claim of proof ("We found it.", "We tested it") or a bare question ("How?"). Inside the video, speech starts at second 0 in all 12. The opening is either a big claim or a dated cold-open story, and there's no channel intro. A video then keeps viewers by stacking problems. Each answer exposes a new obstacle, usually introduced with "But…", "Except there's a problem" or "Wait, what?", roughly every 1–2 minutes. The thumbnail's promise is held back for a long time: the median payoff comes at about 18 minutes, and 7 of 12 videos pay it off in their second half. The pattern is very consistent across the 11 regular explainers. The exception is V3, a 5-minute trailer launching a new channel.

**What this means.** Shots are not short (median ~3.4 s, about 11 cuts a minute). But the picture changes about every 1.5 s, roughly twice as often as it cuts, so animation, zooms and overlays carry much of the visual pace. The openings split into two kinds. Videos that open on a big claim cut fast (14–16 cuts in 30 s in [V1](https://youtu.be/eyZEqSPeMwg), [V6](https://youtu.be/wt4p2oalmRY), [V8](https://youtu.be/GK2pZ_oVU1o)). Videos that open on a dated story cut slowly (3 cuts in [V2](https://youtu.be/NiCgcUTL9_Q), 1 in [V9](https://youtu.be/mvcesPWvUIc), 7 in [V10](https://youtu.be/8HBDE-msUjw) and [V11](https://youtu.be/tz23G_UXCGA)) and lean on narration over calm animation. Speech is fast and starts immediately. In 8 of 12 videos the first 10 seconds are spoken faster than the video's overall average, with the highest at 234 wpm in [V6 0:00](https://youtu.be/wt4p2oalmRY?t=0).

## Script language
**Tone.** Conversational, curious, enthusiastic but precise. The narrator speaks in first person ("we", "let's"), admits doubt ("personally, I tend to think" [V7](https://youtu.be/tL9Lw250spc)), and adds live reactions and light humour.

**Techniques:**
- Short punch sentences after long explanations; the median sentence is 12.5 words.
- "But / Except / Unfortunately" turns.
- Exact numbers used as stakes.
- Rhetorical questions that open each section.
- "You might think…" setups.
- Named people and dates.
- Callback closes ("You have arrived at your destination." [V12](https://youtu.be/kS-CGkiPetQ)).

## Limits
